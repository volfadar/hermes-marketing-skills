# Making a Business Direction Specific

## Specificity is evidence work

Use niche-down questions to turn vague history into testable detail. Do not keep shrinking an audience until the phrase sounds unique. A narrow phrase without demand, access, proof, or useful differentiation is not a position.

## Five ladders

Move down the ladder that is still vague:

| Ladder | Broad | Specific |
|---|---|---|
| Task | “marketing” | rebuilt marketplace variants and return SOP |
| Buyer | “UMKM” | owner-operated fashion sellers with 50–300 monthly orders |
| Problem | “sales low” | wrong-variant returns consume owner support time |
| Outcome | “lebih rapi” | fewer avoidable return cases and faster response |
| Proof | “pernah admin” | 12 months handling X listings; SOP/screenshot available |
| Context | “online” | Shopee stores using chat manually during campaign peaks |

Ask one narrowing question, then capture the answer in the proof ledger.

## Patterns by business type

### Service or high-ticket

Specify:

```text
reachable buyer × costly decision/problem × credible outcome × proof
```

The buyer must be able to pay and the proof must match the promised outcome.

### Commodity or same-product offer

Do not default to demographic niching. Specify:

```text
use occasion or buying friction
× small operational/product change
× efficient distribution
× contribution economics
```

Examples of possible levers: fit assurance, replenishment ritual, workplace bundle, installation, curation, discreet form factor, local availability, service recovery. Research before choosing.

### Experienced professional

Specify:

```text
repeated case pattern × person currently at that point × mechanism/proof
```

A title such as “accountant” is background. The repeated high-value decision they can help with is the opportunity.

### Creative professional

Specify:

```text
buyer context × decision the work changes × distinctive process/proof
```

“For creators” is often too broad and may say nothing about why the work is chosen.

### Two-sided or partner market

Specify both:

```text
partner's incentive and activation job
× end user's problem and buying journey
```

Do not assume the partner benefits just because the end user does.

## Research gate

Before treating a specific direction as viable, check:

1. Do buyers describe the problem in public evidence or interviews?
2. What do they use now, including doing nothing?
3. Is the problem costly/annoying enough for the proposed commitment?
4. Can the user reach these buyers through an existing or affordable route?
5. Does the price/margin support acquisition and delivery?
6. What proof or operating change makes the claim credible?
7. What finding would kill the idea?

If evidence is thin, output a research question or experiment—not a niche declaration.

## Output

Produce two or three candidate directions:

```markdown
### Direction
- Buyer/context:
- Specific job:
- User proof/access:
- Current alternative:
- Evidence:
- Economic assumption:
- Main unknown:
- Test:
```

Pass candidates to `positioning-lab.md`. Do not turn the candidate directly into a final “Saya membantu…” line.
