# Excavation — finding what she already has before deciding what she should do

## Why this file exists

A recorded session, 19 August 2026, Telegram, `muse-spark-1.2`. A user opened with
*"saya sudah punya pembuatan website, walau hanya vibe coding, jualnya gimana ya"* and
left 55 minutes later saying *"asli gajelas banget, AI slop banget rekomendasinya,
tidak helpful."*

Nothing the coach said was false. It correctly refused to invent a price. It correctly
separated take-home from revenue. Every guardrail in this skill held. And the session was
still worthless, because across fourteen turns the coach asked **only about limits and
transactions**: the target number, how many clients, how much time, how many people he could
contact, which format he preferred, what he had promoted. Fourteen turns, and it never once
asked who he was.

What it walked past:

| He said | What was sitting in it | What the coach did |
|---|---|---|
| *"pulang ngantor"* | He has a job. A job is an industry, a boss, colleagues, vendors, and their customers. | Recorded `ACCESS: none`. Concluded he had no route to business owners. |
| *"baru lulus kuliah"* | A campus. A cohort. Lecturers. A final project. An alumni group that is four months old and still warm. | Used it only to excuse having no experience. |
| *"typeberry.co sama msnschool.id"* | Two shipped, live, domain-bought products — one of them apparently for a **school**. Somebody's need, somewhere. | Called them *"iseng"*, agreed they were "bukti bisa bikin", asked which one to use as portfolio, never asked why either exists. |
| *"gapunya temen pengusaha"* | A claim about his address book, not about his reach. | Accepted verbatim as a hard ceiling. |
| *"saya introvert"* | An energy constraint on **format**, not on volume. | Converted into a cap of three people per week and built the whole plan around it. |
| *"maunya 100jt"* | An anchor that came from somewhere. Nobody names 100 juta at random. | Treated purely as an unverified price and blocked. |

`msnschool.id` is the clearest one. A school website, built by someone who just left school,
who works in an office and says he knows no business owners. Four questions — whose school,
who asked, did anyone else see it, is there a teacher who knows you built it — is either a
warm buyer, a testimonial, a referral route into every other school in the city, and a
positioning territory nobody else in the freelance-website market can claim, **or** it is
genuinely nothing, and now you know instead of guessing. The coach asked none of them, and
then told him to go find three strangers.

**The guardrails in this skill stop the coach from lying. This file is what stops it from
being useless.** They are separate problems and the second one is not solved by tightening
the first.

---

## The doctrine

> Every person arrives holding more than they will report. What they report is what they
> think counts. The job is to find what they did not think counted.

Three consequences, and each contradicts something a coach naturally wants to do:

1. **"Saya nggak punya apa-apa" is a self-assessment, never a finding.** It is what a
   beginner says because they are comparing themselves to a professional. Accepting it ends
   the session before it starts. Answer it by asking about a period of their life, never by
   asking the same question again in softer words.
2. **Excavate before you constrain.** Limits are real and they belong in `batasan`, but a
   limit collected before an asset becomes the whole plan's shape. The recorded session
   collected five limits and zero assets, and produced a plan made entirely of limits: three
   people, one hour, one voice note. Ask what they have first. Ask what stops them second.
3. **The interesting material never arrives as an answer to a business question.** It
   arrives sideways, in an aside, in a domain name, in a throwaway *"dulu sih pernah…"*.
   Listening for it is the skill; the question bank below only creates the openings.

## The ten seams

Work these in roughly this order for someone pre-revenue. For an owner with a running
business, seams 1, 6, 7 and 9 still apply and usually pay best — an established seller has
just as much unexamined history, and it is where `sikap.kenapa_aku` actually comes from.

**One seam per turn. Never a list.** Each seam below gives the opening question, the
follow-up that converts an answer into something usable, and what the answer unlocks.

---

### Seam 1 — Where they already stand every day

The highest-yield seam and the one most often skipped, because it does not look like
business history.

> *"Sehari-hari kerja di bidang apa? Kantor tempat kamu kerja itu jualan apa ke siapa?"*

Follow-up, choose one:

> *"Di kantor, kamu paling sering ngobrol sama bagian apa?"*
> *"Kantor kamu pakai vendor luar buat apa aja?"*
> *"Kalau ada yang bingung soal <hal teknis di bidangnya>, biasanya larinya ke siapa?"*

**Unlocks:** an industry they already speak the language of; a set of real buyers (their
employer's vendors *are* small businesses that sell to companies); coworkers who have side
businesses, spouses with side businesses, and relatives; and the single most valuable thing
a beginner can have — a reason a stranger would take them seriously in one specific room.

**Failure to avoid:** hearing "kantor" and filing it under `CAP: waktu`. A job is a time cap
*and* an access asset. The recorded session took the cap and threw away the asset.

---

### Seam 2 — Family trade

> *"Orang tua kerjanya apa? Ada saudara — om, tante, sepupu — yang punya usaha?"*

Follow-up:

> *"Kamu pernah ikut bantu nggak? Bagian apa yang kamu pegang?"*
> *"Sejak kecil sering lihat/dengar soal apa di rumah?"*

**Unlocks:** category familiarity nobody paid for and nobody can fake. Someone who grew up
in a workshop knows what a workshop owner worries about at 4pm on a Friday. That is worth
more in a sales conversation than a portfolio. It also unlocks the first buyer: a relative's
business is a real business with a real problem, and it is warm.

**Careful:** familiarity is not permission and it is not proof of demand. "Bapak saya punya
bengkel" makes bengkel a *credible segment to test*, not a validated market. It changes what
you test first, not what you claim.

---

### Seam 3 — Campus, school, and the role the group gave them

> *"Kuliah/sekolah jurusan apa? Skripsi atau tugas akhirnya soal apa?"*

Then the question that actually matters:

> *"Kalau lagi tugas kelompok, kamu biasanya kebagian apa? Yang bagian itu kamu yang minta atau otomatis dikasih?"*

**Unlocks:** the second answer is a competence signal that did not come from self-report.
Being handed the slides every time, or the coding, or the negotiating with the lecturer, is
a group of peers voting on what someone is reliably good at — repeatedly, over years,
without being asked to be nice about it. It is the closest thing a fresh graduate has to a
performance review.

Also unlocks: a cohort of a few dozen people who graduated the same month, lecturers, a
campus organisation, and alumni — a network that is at its warmest right now and decays
fast.

---

### Seam 4 — Communities, including the ones that do not look like communities

> *"Ada komunitas atau grup yang kamu ikutin? Nggak harus formal — grup WA, Discord, klan game, komunitas hobi, pengajian, alumni, apa aja."*

If the answer is "nggak ada":

> *"Grup WA di HP kamu sekarang ada berapa kira-kira? Yang paling sering bunyi grup apa?"*

**Unlocks:** distribution. "Nggak punya circle" is almost never literally true; it usually
means "saya nggak kenal orang penting". A person with no professional network still sits
inside four to ten group chats, and each is a room full of people with jobs, families and
small businesses. Communities also carry an identity lever for positioning — see
`positioning-lab.md`, *Identity/community*.

**Failure to avoid:** treating a community as a place to broadcast an offer. It is a place
where someone already knows them, which is a completely different asset. Do not turn seam 4
into a spam plan; that also breaks `funnel-ethics.md`.

---

### Seam 5 — Where free time and their own money go

> *"Di luar kerjaan, waktu luang kamu paling sering habis buat apa?"*
> *"Setahun terakhir, kamu beli apa yang orang lain mungkin bilang 'ngapain beli gituan'?"*

**Unlocks:** two different things. Free time shows what they will still be doing in week six
without being paid — the same signal Stage 1's completion question is after, sourced from
somewhere it actually exists for a beginner. Money spent shows a category where **they are
the buyer**, so they already know the language, the alternatives, the frustrations, and what
a purchase feels like from the inside. A category you have personally paid into is the
cheapest market research anyone will ever have.

---

### Seam 6 — The existing trail

Everything they have already made, shipped, organised, ran, or finished — explicitly
including things that made no money.

> *"Ada nggak yang pernah kamu bikin sampai jadi? Nggak harus yang dibayar — bikin web, ngurus acara, jualan kecil-kecilan, ngedit video, ngurus grup, apa aja."*

Then, for **each** item, and this is where the recorded session lost the most:

> *"<nama itu> itu buat siapa? Kenapa kamu bikin?"*
> *"Ada yang minta, atau kamu inisiatif sendiri?"*
> *"Ada yang pernah lihat/pakai? Reaksinya gimana?"*
> *"Masih jalan sampai sekarang?"*

**Unlocks:** the origin story is the asset, not the artifact. *"Iseng"* is the answer people
give about things they did for free; it is almost never true. Someone chose that problem,
that name, that audience, and bought a domain for it. **Buying a domain is a decision with a
price on it.** Find out whose problem it was.

**Rule: never accept "iseng" as a stopping point.** It is a filler word, and one follow-up
almost always converts it into a person, a request, or a frustration.

---

### Seam 7 — The role people always give them

> *"Kalau temen atau keluarga lagi butuh bantuan, kamu paling sering dimintain tolong soal apa?"*

Follow-up:

> *"Yang terakhir minta tolong itu siapa, buat apa?"*
> *"Pernah nggak ada yang nawarin bayar, terus kamu tolak/gratisin?"*

**Unlocks:** unpriced demand that already exists. This is the strongest signal available to
someone with zero commercial history, because other people initiated it. Nobody asks for
help with something you are bad at, twice. The second follow-up sometimes surfaces an actual
willingness-to-pay event that the person has never counted as one — someone offered money
and they refused out of embarrassment. Under `hermes-discipline.md` Rule 5 that is not money
received, but it is far above "said it sounds interesting", and it names a real buyer.

---

### Seam 8 — Idols and references

> *"Ada nggak orang yang kamu ikutin terus — di YouTube, di X, di IG — yang kalau dia ngepost kamu pasti baca?"*
> *"Apa yang bikin kamu suka sama cara dia?"*

**Unlocks:** direction. What someone admires is the most honest available statement of the
kind of operator they are willing to become, and it predicts what they will sustain far
better than a personality score. It also names the reference class for tone, format, and
price bracket, and it is often the source of an otherwise inexplicable number — the person
who says *"maunya 100jt"* usually saw somebody charge it.

**The move when a number appears:**

> *"100 juta itu kamu lihat di mana? Ada yang pernah kamu lihat dapet segitu?"*

That question costs one turn and converts a blocked, unverified price into a piece of
evidence: which market they were looking at, which operator, which segment, and therefore
what the actual transfer gap is. The recorded session blocked the number four separate times
and never asked where it came from.

**Careful:** an admired operator's market is not the user's market. Use it as a lead and a
tone reference, never as a benchmark — this is exactly the segment-transfer gap in
`market-adaptation.md`.

---

### Seam 9 — The grievance

> *"Ada nggak hal di bidang kamu yang bikin kamu mikir 'kok gini banget sih, harusnya bisa lebih bener'?"*

**Unlocks:** the single richest source of `sikap.kenapa_aku` and the most defensible
positioning lever, because it is an opinion with a person behind it. A stance that came from
irritation is the one thing a competitor cannot copy by rewriting a caption, and it survives
the swap test in `scripts/lib/copycheck.py` automatically.

It also converts directly into an exclusion — *"who it is not for"* on the territory card —
which most positioning work never gets an honest answer to.

---

### Seam 10 — The refusals, last

Only after the seams above have produced something. This is Stage 2's constraint register,
and it belongs at the end of excavation rather than the start.

> *"Ada yang kamu udah tau kamu nggak mau lakuin, walaupun hasilnya bagus?"*

**Why last:** a limit is a boundary drawn around an asset. Drawn first, it is just a wall.
The recorded session drew five walls and never found the room.

## Reading "gapunya" correctly

Beginners state limits in absolute terms. All four of these are reports about how someone
feels, and none of them is the fact it sounds like.

| What they say | What it usually means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| *"gapunya temen pengusaha"* | "None of my friends are entrepreneurs." | Their friends have parents, bosses, and relatives who are. Their employer has vendors. Ask about the second ring, not the first. |
| *"gapunya circle"* | "I don't network." | Ask how many group chats are on their phone. |
| *"cuma sejam"* | Real, and a format constraint. | One focused hour is roughly twenty industry hours a year. It rules out volume tactics; it does not rule out one deep relationship a week. |
| *"saya introvert"* | "Cold approaches cost me a lot." | Constrains the **format** (warm over cold, written or voice over crowds, one-to-one over one-to-many). It does not set a headcount. The recorded session turned it into a hard cap of three people and then designed to that cap. |

Never argue with any of these. Ask the reframing question instead and let them find the
counter-example themselves — it holds, and an argument does not.

## From fragment to asset

A fragment is only worth collecting if it can become one of three things. Name which, out
loud in your own reasoning, before moving on:

| Becomes | Test | Example from the recorded session |
|---|---|---|
| **ACCESS** — a reachable buyer route | Can he name a specific person or room this reaches? | `msnschool.id` → the teacher/admin who received it → every other school in the city |
| **PROOF** — credible familiarity or reason to believe | Would a buyer's risk drop on hearing it? | Two live sites with real domains, shipped alone, working around a full-time job |
| **SEGMENT** — a buyer world he already speaks | Does he know its words, prices, and complaints without research? | Whatever industry his employer is in; whatever `typeberry` was built for |

A fragment that becomes none of the three is background colour. Record it in `asal.cerita`
if it is a story worth telling, and move on without ceremony.

## Benang merah — the synthesis

**Only after roughly eight to twelve fragments across at least five seams.** Earlier than
that, any thread you find is a pattern imposed on noise, and the user will feel it as
flattery.

The method:

1. Lay the fragments out. Look for what recurs in **three or more** of them — a recurring
   audience, a recurring kind of problem, a recurring role he takes, a recurring
   irritation. The thread is the repetition, not the most impressive single item.
2. Write it in **his words**, assembled from things he actually said. Not a slogan, not a
   job title, not "kamu adalah seorang X".
3. **Read it back as a question, never as a revelation.**

   > *"Aku lihat polanya: <thread, in his words>. Kalau ditarik gitu, kena nggak? Atau ada yang meleset?"*

4. If he corrects it, the correction is better data than your version was. Take it verbatim.
5. If nothing recurs, **say so plainly.** *"Belum kelihatan polanya, masih kepingan-kepingan.
   Aku gali satu lagi ya."* An invented thread is worse than no thread, for exactly the
   reason `profile.py` refuses a copied `sikap`: it becomes a sentence he repeats about
   himself that was never true.

Once accepted, the thread is written to `asal.benang_merah` and it becomes the **primary
proof lever** for positioning — it feeds `sikap.kenapa_aku` and the *Personal proof* and
*Career adjacency* rows in `positioning-lab.md`.

### Worked example, from the real transcript

Fragments available by turn nine, all of which the coach actually had:

```
kerja kantoran (industry unknown — never asked)
baru lulus kuliah (major unknown — never asked)
bisa bikin web sendiri, vibe coding
typeberry.co — live, jadi (purpose unknown — never asked)
msnschool.id — live, jadi, sekolah (purpose unknown — never asked)
lebih nyaman voice note daripada nulis panjang
introvert
angka 100jt muncul dari suatu tempat (never asked)
```

Three of those eight point at the same place — a fresh graduate, a school website, and a
campus he left four months ago. That is not proof of a market. It is a **segment worth one
week's test that costs nothing**, with a warm first contact, a live demo built for exactly
that buyer, and language he speaks natively because he sat in it until this year.

What the coach recommended instead: contact three strangers whose websites look bad.

Notice what the difference is not. It is not a loosened guardrail — there is still no price,
no claimed demand, no invented benchmark, and the verdict on schools-as-a-market is
`plausible-test-only` in exactly the sense `market-adaptation.md` defines. The difference is
that the test aims at a door he can already open.

## Anti-loop

The recorded session presented the same three-option menu — *langsung bayar / diagnosa
gratis / diagnosa dibayar* — in four separate turns, and re-summarised the same five facts
at the top of six consecutive replies. That is what "bolak balik mulu" was.

Hard rules:

1. **Never present the same option set twice.** If he did not pick from it the first time,
   the menu was not the problem — the missing input was. Go find the input.
2. **Re-state accumulated facts only when they change, or when he asks.** A recap at the top
   of every turn reads as stalling, and on a small model it becomes the reply's default
   filler. One line, only for the fact that just changed: *"Oke, catat: 10 juta itu bersih."*
3. **Every turn must do one of two things:** record something new, or open a seam not yet
   opened. If neither happened last turn, you are looping — change seam, do not rephrase.
4. **Two refusals on the same axis means the axis is wrong.** Twice blocked on price? Stop
   asking about price and ask where the number came from (seam 8). Twice blocked on
   outreach? Stop asking how many people and ask which rooms he is already in (seams 1–4).
5. **Never apologise twice for the same thing.** The recorded session apologised for going
   in circles and then, in the same message, went in the same circle. Change the question,
   not the tone.

The mechanical check: if your reply contains a list of options that appeared in an earlier
reply, delete it and ask a seam question instead.

## What excavation is not

- **Not therapy.** Ask about facts and events, not feelings about them. *"Bapak kerja apa"*
  is a fact question. *"Gimana perasaan kamu soal bapak"* is not this skill's business.
- **Not flattery.** Do not tell someone their hobby is a hidden superpower. Find where it
  connects to a buyer, or record it and move on. `conversation-and-dossier.md` already bans
  manufactured breakthroughs; this is the same rule with more material to be tempted by.
- **Not a licence to invent demand.** Excavation produces `ACCESS`, `PROOF` and `SEGMENT`
  — all of which are facts about the user. It produces **no** buyer-side evidence. A warm
  contact is a cheaper test, never a validated market. Everything in `market-adaptation.md`
  still applies, unchanged, to every claim about money.
- **Not an interrogation.** One question per turn, 40–120 words, in his register. Ten seams
  over ten turns is a conversation. Ten seams in one message is a form, and a form is where
  he leaves.
- **Not a prerequisite for urgent work.** If he arrives with a customer waiting and a price
  question, answer it. Excavate afterwards, or in the gaps.

```yaml
    - nama: ""
      untuk_siapa: ""      # never leave this blank on the strength of "iseng"
      kenapa: ""
      masih_jalan: ""

  keresahan: ""            # seam 9 — the grievance

```

`batasan` (seam 10) stays where it is. Read `asal` back before every recommendation, the same
way `batasan` is read back — a plan that uses none of what he has is generic by construction,
and generic is the complaint this file exists to answer.
