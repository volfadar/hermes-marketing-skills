# Funnel Architecture, Economics, and Consent

## Start with constraints

A funnel is a route system, not a four-box content template. Do not design it before learning:

- business goal and core offer;
- monthly cash budget and acceptable payback period;
- owner/team hours and fulfillment capacity;
- price, contribution margin, cash cycle, refund/return risk, retention/repeat;
- buying cycle, decision-makers, and trust required;
- current audience, customer list, content, partners, locations, and brand equity;
- legal, religious, platform, safety, and consent constraints.

If data is unknown, label the assumption and create a measurement step. If budget is withheld, compare scenarios instead of prescribing one plan.

## Design backward

1. Define the economic event that matters: first purchase, qualified demo, subscription activation, partner activation, repeat purchase, or parent-brand discovery.
2. Identify the proof/risk reduction needed immediately before that event.
3. Add the lightest credible commitment that can produce that proof.
4. Continue backward to the contexts where each segment already pays attention.
5. Add post-purchase success, expansion, referral, and churn prevention.

## Architecture choices

### Value ladder

Use when trust and capability must be experienced gradually.

```text
useful free tool/content
  → low-risk diagnostic or event
  → low-ticket starter
  → guided implementation/pilot
  → core offer
  → continuity or advanced offer
```

Each step must deliver standalone value. A teaser that withholds the useful part is not a bridge.

### Parallel acquisition routes

Use when different contexts discover the same core offer.

```text
search/problem-aware route ─┐
community/identity route ───┼→ shared proof experience → core offer
partner/referral route ─────┘
```

Give each route its own message and economics. Do not duplicate the same generic content everywhere.

### Two-market system

Use when one segment distributes or sells to another.

```text
partner/affiliate/sales route:
recruit → qualify → enable → first referred buyer → active partner → retained partner
                                      │
                                      ▼
end-user route:
discover → low-risk value → proof experience → core offer → success → referral
```

Measure partner recruitment and end-user conversion separately. A large affiliate list with no activated partners is not traction.

### Child brand to parent brand

Use only when a focused entry brand can enter a context the parent cannot credibly enter, and the team can fund separate operations.

```text
entry brand solves a narrow occasion
  → customer experiences a distinctive product/program
  → transparent endorsed-by/portfolio connection
  → relevant parent-company discovery
  → cross-sell only with clear consent and fit
```

Do not create a fake-independent brand to manipulate curiosity. Make ownership discoverable, prevent confusion, and calculate the cost of two identities, channels, and content systems.

### B2B trust network

Use when the buyer is offline, risk-averse, or committee-driven.

```text
trusted node/partner
  → problem diagnosis
  → proof using buyer's data/context
  → limited pilot
  → stakeholder review
  → commercial rollout
  → adoption and expansion
```

Map user, economic buyer, approver, blocker, implementer, and recommender separately.

## Bridge selection

A bridge earns the next commitment by reducing one specific risk:

| Risk | Possible bridge |
|---|---|
| “Not for me” | diagnostic, fit checker, guided selector |
| “I do not believe it” | live demo, sample, before/after evidence, trusted endorsement |
| “Too hard” | setup clinic, template, done-with-you starter |
| “Too expensive” | low-scope paid pilot, calculator, transparent value model |
| “I will regret it” | trial boundary, fit guarantee, easy exit, service recovery |
| “I am not ready” | event, challenge, waitlist, educational sequence with a next decision |

For high-friction core offers, use multiple bridges only when each removes a different material risk. More steps can lower conversion and raise cost.

**Every bridge carries a price or a stated reason it is free.** A ladder without prices is a
content plan. A free bridge is legitimate to build proof; it cannot measure willingness to pay.
Do not turn an expert method into a paid rung merely because suppliers sell it elsewhere. Read
`market-adaptation.md`: compare a direct paid outcome, diagnosis bundled inside implementation,
and a separately paid diagnosis. The third shape is justified only by matching buyer-side demand
or a purchase context that requires it. Each priced step must buy a distinct outcome or decision,
not the same work under a new name.

**Stop/scale rules key on the top of the demand ladder** (`hermes-discipline.md` Rule 5): money
received, a deposit, a scheduled paid pilot, or a written commitment naming scope and price.
A rule such as "continue if 2 of 3 contacts mention a price" measures politeness — doubly so
when the work is then delivered free. That exact design was proposed in a recorded session.

**One good route beats three thin ones.** When the operator is one person with a hard cash cap,
prefer a single well-built route and say why you declined the others, with the condition that
would re-open them. Two models correctly refused to invent a consumer market and explained the
refusal; that is better architecture than a diagram with more arrows.

## When evidence lands that breaks the plan

A plan meets contradicting news often enough that the response needs a shape. The failure mode
is patching: the new fact is acknowledged in a sentence, a route is added to compensate, and the
original target survives untouched because nobody did the subtraction.

**Any revision names what stops, before it names what starts.** Three lists, in this order:

```text
Stop      : <what we are no longer doing, and why it is now pointless>
Keep      : <what still holds, and the assumption it still rests on>
Start     : <what replaces the stopped work, sized against the hole>
```

Then redo the goal reconciliation from `economics-and-goal-fit.md` with the new numbers. A
target that survived the news without being recomputed did not survive it — it was just not
looked at.

**Harvest what is about to disappear.** When the news involves a person leaving — the sponsor
retires, the manager transfers, the contract lapses — there is a short window in which
introductions, a written reference, and the account history are still obtainable, and after it
closes they are not. That window is usually the most valuable item on the list and it is almost
never on it. Put it first, with its expiry date.

**Attack the assumption behind the decision, once, and then stop.** A customer bringing work
in-house is making a bet about their own capability. It is legitimate to name what that bet
usually breaks on — specialised equipment, after-hours emergencies, spare-part supply, the
person who actually knows the system — and to offer a smaller shape that covers exactly that
residue. It is not legitimate to keep selling the old contract to someone who has decided.

## Budget scenarios

When exact numbers are unavailable:

| Scenario | Constraint | Typical emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Lean | owner time, little cash | interviews, partnerships, manual service, one channel |
| Standard | repeatable monthly budget | one paid test plus owned nurture and conversion assets |
| Aggressive | team and risk capital | parallel routes, events/partners, paid acquisition, stronger measurement |

State actual assumed rupiah, hours, and capacity for each scenario. “Aggressive” is not permission to waste money.

## Economics per node

For each node record:

- segment and route;
- job and risk reduced;
- offer/experience;
- acquisition source;
- owner and capacity;
- cash and time cost;
- numerator/denominator metric;
- next node;
- stop/scale rule.

Useful formulas:

```text
contribution per order = price - product cost - platform/payment fee
                         - fulfillment - expected returns/support

allowable CAC = contribution during chosen payback window

route yield = entrants × step-1 rate × step-2 rate × ... × core conversion

partner productivity = active partners with qualified referrals / recruited partners
```

Use ranges until real data exists. Never import a conversion benchmark without a source and contextual caveat.

## Measurement

Track the smallest set that reveals the broken link:

- reach/context → qualified attention;
- attention → first commitment;
- bridge start → bridge completion;
- completion → core conversion;
- activation/time-to-value;
- repeat/retention/churn;
- referral or partner activation;
- contribution margin and payback;
- opt-out, complaint, refund, and block rates.

Pre-register kill and scale rules against the user's real volume and economics. Example: after
the agreed test cap, stop this message if no matching buyer makes the agreed commercial
commitment; interview non-buyers before spending on traffic. Numbers are inputs from the user or
tagged test assumptions, never imported defaults.

## Consent and human control

- Contact capture requires explicit, contextual opt-in.

**"They already bought from me" is not opt-in, and it is the rationalisation that gets
past this rule.** An address collected to deliver a file, an invoice, a receipt, or a
booking confirmation was given for *that* purpose. Being a paying customer makes a
promotional message more welcome; it does not make it consented to, and in Indonesia
UU PDP treats purpose limitation as the point. The honest move is to say so and design
around it: mail the existing list once about *their own purchase* with a genuine opt-in
for anything further, and treat the reply rate as the size of the real list.

Watch for the same shape elsewhere — *"they're in my WhatsApp group"*, *"they follow
me"*, *"they gave me their card"*. Each is a relationship, and none is a permission.
- Explain what messages the person will receive and how often.
- Provide a working opt-out.
- Keep affiliate/sponsor/ownership disclosure clear.
- Do not scrape private contact data or add people to broadcasts without permission.
- Segment messages by the reason for opt-in.
- A human reviews and triggers sales messages, exceptions, and high-stakes claims.
- Do not use false urgency, hidden ownership, fabricated proof, or manipulative diagnosis.
