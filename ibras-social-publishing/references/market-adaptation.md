# Market Adaptation — fit the recommendation to the buyer, not a country stereotype

<!-- CANONICAL SOURCE: shared/references/market-adaptation.md
     Synced into every skill by shared/sync.sh. Edit the canonical copy only. -->

This layer is required whenever a skill recommends a market, position, paid offer,
funnel, acquisition channel, or automation spend. It is deliberately reusable: the
same checks apply to a food seller in Surabaya, an employee starting a side service,
a Jakarta B2B consultant, or a buyer outside Indonesia.

It does **not** say “Indonesia always uses WhatsApp”, “low-budget buyers never buy
advice”, or “audit never sells”. Any of those may be true in a particular case and
false in the next one. The job is to establish which case this is.

## First resolve whose money it is

A sentence such as “income saya Rp5–10 juta” is ambiguous. Before calculating or
choosing an offer, separate:

- **personal salary** or other household income;
- **business revenue** (sales before costs);
- **contribution, profit, and take-home** from the operator's business;
- the **buyer budget** available to purchase this category;
- the operator's cash/time cap for the experiment.

Do not interrogate. Ask only the ambiguity that would change the next action. A
statutory business-size label is useful for regulation; it is not proof that two
buyers share awareness, purchasing power, or buying behaviour.

## Market-fit card

Complete this card internally before recommending a commercial route. Unknown is a
valid value; silently filling a blank is not.

```text
MARKET FIT
  geography             : where this buyer actually buys
  buyer + scale         : role, business/personal context, B2B/B2C, ability to pay
  purchase context      : trigger, urgency, who approves, and normal buying channel
  category language     : words buyers use; awareness of this method/category
  current alternative   : what they do/pay for now, including free/manual substitutes
  buyer-side signals    : enquiries, purchases, budgets, tenders, reviews, interviews
  seller-side supply    : offers/listings found; never counted as buyer demand
  segment transfer gap  : what differs from the evidence population
  contradictions        : reasons the route may not sell or may not fit
  verdict               : validated | plausible-test-only | unverified | contradicted
```

For an early conversation, ask only enough to fill the decision-changing blanks. A
long form is not a prerequisite to help someone take a small reversible step.

## Evidence rules

### Supply is not demand

A seller page, agency menu, marketplace listing, or consultant price proves that a
supplier offers something. It does not prove the target buyer knows the category,
wants it, or has paid for it. Record seller/vendor pages under **seller-side supply**.
Prefer buyer-side signals such as a completed purchase, paid pilot, tender/brief,
budget named in a real buying conversation, renewal, buyer review, or repeated
request in the target context.

### Geography match is not segment match

Evidence from the same country can still have a **segment transfer** gap. Corporate
procurement for an investigation cannot validate demand from a micro retailer; a
creator audience cannot validate an industrial buyer; a US benchmark cannot set an
Indonesian price. Such evidence may explain a mechanism. It cannot establish the
target segment's purchase behaviour without a matching buyer-side signal.

### A rename is not adaptation

Changing “audit” to “health check”, “assessment”, “investigation”, or a catchy local
name does not create demand. Apply the **rename test**: remove the label and ask what
the buyer receives, what they can do next, and why they would pay now. If the work and
purchase reason did not change, the market problem remains.

### Verdict controls the strength of the recommendation

- **validated** — matching buyer-side commercial evidence exists. It may be a paid
  offer or funnel node, with its limits stated.
- **plausible-test-only** — mechanism fits and weak/local signals exist, but paid
  demand is not established. Offer a small capped test, never a market claim.
- **unverified** — decision-changing evidence is missing. Research or ask; do not
  install it as positioning.
- **contradicted** — matching evidence points against it. Stop, replace, or change
  the segment; do not merely rename it.

The bar rises with irreversibility and spend. A reversible message test needs less
evidence than building a new service line, buying software, or hiring a team.

## Shape the offer around the outcome

When buyers do not already seek the expert method, compare at least these shapes:

1. **direct outcome** — sell the concrete result or next decision;
2. **bundled diagnosis** — do the diagnosis internally as part of setup or delivery;
3. **separately paid diagnosis** — use only when matching buyer-side evidence supports
   payment for that method/category, or a legal/procurement context requires it.

This is not a ban on audits, coaching, workshops, research, or consulting. An internal
content audit of the owner's own records is always legitimate; it is performance work,
not evidence that strangers will buy an audit. A required statutory audit is a different
purchase context from an optional business diagnostic. An established paid advisory
market remains valid when the matching evidence is present.

## Channel and tool choice

Start from the last real buyers: where did they discover, ask, compare, order, and
pay? Choose the narrowest channel that reaches the next credible buyers and fits the
operator's ACCESS, REFUSE, CAP, time, and buyer budget.

Manual/native/free is the baseline when it can produce the same learning. Automation
earns its place only after consistent activity creates a measured bottleneck or a
business signal such as qualified enquiries, quotes, orders, contribution, renewal,
or saved labour with a clear payback. Do not optimise reach or engagement while the
commercial link is unknown.

## Output contract

Recommendations stay short and useful. Tell the owner:

1. the next action;
2. why it fits *their* evidence and constraints;
3. what remains unknown;
4. the cheapest way to learn it;
5. what result means continue, change, or stop.

Do not print the entire market-fit card unless the user asks for the working notes.
